The four questions everyone asks

dbt already does this.

It does part of it, and its own documentation states the limits: contracts cover SQL models only, sources, seeds and snapshots cannot be contracted at all, and enforcement is not uniform — on Redshift and Snowflake only not_null is enforced, while Postgres enforces every constraint type. If you run both, your enforcement is silently weaker on two platforms and nothing told you. dbt contracts also never cross the wire to a Kafka consumer, and are not a document a reviewer can read.

Why not just use a chat model directly?

For one table, you should — and we will say so. A frontier model writes plausible DDL, a plausible dbt project and a plausible contract. What it will not tell you is which of the three fails to parse. It also leaves no egress record, no residency guarantee, and no version-to-version diff. The claim here is about the fiftieth artifact and the second version, not the first.

Our modellers use erwin and will not switch.

Then don't. We are not asking for the canvas. We are the stage after it — reverse-engineer the warehouse erwin forward-engineered into, and emit what erwin does not: the dbt project, the semantic layer, the contracts, the seed data, the governance lint. The modeller's model stays the source of truth.

We do not trust a language model with our schema.

We will not try to argue you out of that. Pick your position and we will show you the evidence for it: fully air-gapped, where every task resolves to a local runtime and cloud providers are stripped at route resolution — or governed, where every request is written to an append-only ledger before it is sent, and if the ledger cannot be written the request is not made. That is the distinction worth keeping: the ledger is an authorisation record, not a log. A log tells you what happened. This decides whether it happens.

Ask us to break it in front of you

The weakness of a correctness claim is that its value is counterfactual — it shows up as the absence of a failure, which is invisible in a demo. So don't let us present the Proof Log. Make us do this instead:

  1. Bring a page of prose from a real programme. Your requirements, not our sample.

  2. Watch it generate — the DDL executing on DuckDB, the dbt project parsing.

  3. Now edit one column type by hand, the way a hurried engineer would, and re-run. Watch the harness go red and name the artifact that broke.

  4. Then put the same prose through a chat model with no harness, feed that output into the same harness, and see what fails.
