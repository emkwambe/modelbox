## Authoritative Marketing Strategy for LinkedIn & Owned Properties

**Objective:** Establish ModelBox AI as the definitive authority in AI-powered data modeling, schema governance, and analytical data architecture. Position the brand alongside enterprise technology leaders (IBM, Oracle, Snowflake) through authoritative, research-backed content.

**Target Audiences:**
1. **Primary:** Data Architects, Analytics Engineers, Data Engineers (practitioners)
2. **Secondary:** CTOs, VP of Data, CDOs (decision-makers)
3. **Tertiary:** Data Consultants, Educators, Enterprise L&D Leaders (influencers)

**Content Pillars:**
- **Authority:** Research-backed, evidence-based thought leadership
- **Education:** Practical, skill-building content that teaches while it sells
- **Vision:** Forward-looking perspectives on the future of data modeling
- **Proof:** Customer stories, benchmarks, and product capabilities

### 2.2 Content Types & Templates

#### Type A: The Provocative Insight (Monday)

**Template:**
```
[Hook: Contrarian or surprising statement]

[Context: 2-3 sentences of setup]

[Evidence: Data point, research finding, or customer observation]

[Implication: What this means for the reader]

[CTA: Question or invitation to comment]

#DataModeling #DataEngineering #AnalyticsEngineering
```

**Examples:**
- "We analyzed 200 job postings. 'Data Modeler' as a standalone title is dying. Here is what is replacing it."
- "Your dbt project has 500 models and zero data contracts. That is not a data platform. That is a liability."
- "The average data team spends 40% of their time firefighting data issues. The top 10% spend 8%. Here is the difference."

#### Type B: The Authoritative Deep Dive (Wednesday)

**Template:**
```
[Headline: Specific, benefit-driven]

[Introduction: Why this matters now]

[Section 1: The Problem]
[Section 2: The Research/Evidence]
[Section 3: The Framework/Solution]
[Section 4: Implementation Steps]
[Section 5: Common Pitfalls]

[Conclusion: Key takeaways]

[CTA: Link to full resource, invitation to try product]
```

**Examples:**
- "The Complete Guide to Semantic Layer Design in 2026"
- "How to Write a Data Contract That Passes a SOC 2 Audit"
- "The Data Quality Engineering Playbook: From Zero to Production"

#### Type C: The Community Engagement (Friday)

**Formats:**

- Questions: "What is the one data quality test you wish you had implemented sooner?"

## Content Production Workflow

### Weekly Sprint

```
Monday: Content planning meeting
  ├─ Review last week's performance
  ├─ Select topics for next week
  └─ Assign writers, designers, speakers

Tuesday–Wednesday: Content creation
  ├─ Long-form articles (writer)
  ├─ LinkedIn posts (founder/product lead)
  ├─ Visual assets (designer)
  └─ Video recording (founder/advisor)

Thursday: Review & approval
  ├─ Technical accuracy review (senior engineer)
  ├─ Brand voice review (marketing lead)
  └─ Legal/compliance review (if needed)

Friday: Publishing & distribution
  ├─ Schedule LinkedIn posts (Buffer/Hootsuite)
  ├─ Publish blog articles
  ├─ Send email newsletters
  └─ Update resource library
```

## Appendix: Content Templates

### Template A: The LinkedIn Authority Post

```
[STATEMENT OF FACT]

[CONTEXT / WHY IT MATTERS]

[EVIDENCE / DATA POINT]

[IMPLICATION FOR READER]

[CALL TO ACTION / QUESTION]

---

Example:

"Data modeling is no longer a standalone job.

We analyzed 200+ job postings for roles involving data modeling. 
Less than 15% were titled 'Data Modeler.' The rest? Data Engineer, 
Analytics Engineer, BI Engineer, Data Architect.

The market has spoken: modeling is a skill, not a title. The 
practitioners who thrive are those who can model AND engineer 
AND govern AND communicate with stakeholders.

If you are building a data team in 2026, do not hire a 'pure 
modeler.' Hire someone who can design a star schema, write the 
dbt model, enforce a data contract, and explain it to the CFO.

What is the most underrated skill on your data team right now?"
```

### Template B: The Blog Pillar Page

```
H1: [Definitive, keyword-rich title]

Introduction (150 words):
- Hook: Surprising statistic or bold claim
- Context: Why this topic matters now
- Promise: What the reader will learn
- TOC: Linked table of contents

Section 1: The Problem (300 words)
- Pain points
- Real-world consequences
- Who is affected

Section 2: The Framework / Solution (800 words)
- Step-by-step approach
- Visual diagrams
- Code examples

Section 3: Implementation (500 words)
- Tools
- Templates
- Common pitfalls

Section 4: Case Study / Proof (300 words)
- Customer example
- Metrics
- Before/after

Conclusion (150 words)
- Key takeaways
- Next steps
- CTA (download, demo, trial)

---
SEO: Target keyword in H1, first paragraph, 2–3 H2s
Meta description: 155 characters, includes target keyword
Internal links: 3–5 to related blog posts
External links: 2–3 to authoritative sources (research, standards)
```

### Template C: The LinkedIn Document (PDF Carousel)

```
Page 1: Bold headline + hook statistic
Page 2: The problem (1 sentence + visual)
Page 3: The research / evidence (chart or data)
Page 4: The framework (diagram)
Page 5: Step 1 (with example)
Page 6: Step 2 (with example)
Page 7: Step 3 (with example)

Design: Clean, minimal, high contrast
Font: Sans-serif, professional (Inter, Helvetica, or system)

Charts: Simple bar/line charts, no 3D effects
```
